Top 5 cyber trends of the past week

1Firewalls and MSP tools turned into footholds: a Check Point zero-day, N-central miners and ScreenConnect attacks

Check Point said attackers who got into Security Gateways through PHP2Shell (CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619) in Mobile Access portals from October 30 used a new zero-day, CVE-2026-123039 (CVSS 7.8, a root dynamic_server daemon listening on an unauthenticated, world-writable local socket in R81 through R82.20), to get root at “a couple of dozen customers,” install the TSGA backdoor and TSProxy, and run a destroy_v6 wiper on a VMware ESXi host, with Israeli organizations hit first and CISA setting a November 16 federal deadline for the hotfix (SK1000362). Remote-management tools were hit at the same time: Huntback found an XMRig miner on 21 N-able N-central servers, 19 of them already on Hotfix 2, after the 4EverAble2Shell chain (including Apache Axis CVE-2026-90436, CVSS 9.8) was published, so N-able told customers to disable the Legacy API and began shipping 2026.4 HF3, while Huntress counted 288 endpoints at 48 organizations compromised through a blank-key login flaw in the third-party RMM+ ScreenConnect extension (1.0.31 and 1.0.32), and Blackpoint found that the SCKit toolkit dropped on them tries to jump to MSP technicians through ScreenConnect CVE-2026-84869 (fixed in 26.6.5), though no successful jump has been seen.

2Patch week: exploited Outlook and Chrome zero-days, an unpatched Windows SYSTEM exploit and four CVSS 10 SAP bugs

Microsoft’s November 10 Patch Tuesday fixed what The Hacker News counts as 1,259 flaws (the second month in a row above 1,000), including the actively exploited Outlook spoofing bug CVE-2026-124194 (CVSS 6.5), in which opening a crafted message file makes Outlook connect to an attacker’s server without warning and can leak the user’s NTLMv2 hash (CISA deadline November 24), and Google patched Chrome’s eighth exploited zero-day of 2026, the XML use-after-free CVE-2026-125350 (CVSS 8.8, fixed in 157.0.8103.50/.51). On November 13 the researcher known as Nightmare Eclipse published SignDrop, an unpatched Windows CLFS logic flaw that abuses Defender’s signature-update RPC to give a standard user SYSTEM on tested Windows 11 26H2 and Server 2025 builds (Microsoft says it is investigating, that Defender blocks it, and that it has seen no exploitation), while SAP fixed four pre-auth CVSS 10.0 flaws, namely the ICM2SHELL RCE pair (CVE-2026-76964, CVE-2026-89126), PROXYGATE (CVE-2026-89103) and the SAML log-in-as-anyone bug CVE-2026-120017, and Citrix patched 10 critical NetScaler bugs including the Console authentication bypass CVE-2026-120869 (CVSS 9.4), none of them reported exploited.

3Police hit malware loaders, dark-web buyers and North Korea’s fake-meeting scheme

An Operation Endgame action announced November 13 took down 235 servers and 257 domains behind Gootloader, CastleRAT and StealC, cleaned Gootloader from 681 hacked websites and recovered about 6.4 million stolen logins from more than 40,000 victims, with the DOJ tying Gootloader to at least 44 U.S. ransomware attacks costing about $28.8 million, while Europol’s Operation TorNado arrested 354 vendors and buyers from the Archetyp, Bohemia, Nemesis, Kingdom and Tor2Door markets in 14 countries (132 in the U.S.) and seized more than €210 million in cash and crypto, 209 firearms and tonnes of drugs. The FBI also seized cloud infrastructure and more than 900 victim webcam recordings used by FaceStealer, a BlueNoroff (APT38) scheme it says stole more than $400 million in crypto over 18 months by inviting targets from hijacked accounts to fake Zoom or Teams calls, playing recorded or deepfake video of people they knew, then pushing a ClickFix-style “audio fix” that installs malware.

4Poisoned code, hardware and updates: wfx-loot on GitHub, implanted Trezor wallets and a German drone vendor

Starting November 10, the wfx-loot campaign used 4,837 hijacked GitHub accounts to open “docs: dependency audit notes” probe pull requests in 61,764 repositories and ran a roughly 1,500-line secret stealer in at least 1,806 of them, taking Actions secrets, cloud and SSH credentials and OpenAI, Anthropic, Copilot and Cursor tokens, then rewriting AWS role trust policies and adding Azure wfx-ci-* federated credentials that survive secret rotation, according to Aikido and GitGuardian. Trezor confirmed on November 12 that three Safe 5 wallets sold by CryptoBilis, a former Southeast Asian reseller, carried an extra circuit board that reads the recovery phrase off the screen and sends it out over the mobile network, and it released firmware 2.13.1 to block the known implants, while German drone maker Quantum Systems said its drones are flying again after the Bundeswehr grounded them because, according to Der Spiegel, a malicious update to its QBase mission-planning software reached customers in late October.

5SilentAir: state-level call and SMS interception from inside Asian mobile networks

Telecom security firm Enea said a newly named state-level actor, SilentAir, hijacked equipment at operators in at least six Asian countries to send SS7 and Diameter location updates that diverted targets’ calls and texts, mostly people in Taiwan including political and government figures, with interception making up 18.3% of its activity (against 2.1% for a typical surveillance company) and some SMS 2FA codes captured right after a diversion took effect. Enea links SilentAir to China only with low confidence, citing a UTC+8 working day and the absence of Chinese targets, says the group is disrupted for now, and separately reports that the China-linked Liminal Panda’s GTPDoor backdoor is in at least 5 to 10 operators, urging carriers to treat the IPX interconnect as untrusted.
