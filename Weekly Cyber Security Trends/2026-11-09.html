Top 5 cyber trends of the past week

1OpenSSH’s SSHole exploited within hours, plus an APT flaw that hands MitM attackers root

OpenSSH 10.6p2 fixes SSHole (CVE-2026-123158, CVSS 8.1), which lets an unauthenticated attacker log in to accounts whose password is disabled (hash “*”) by sending an oversized password on musl-based Linux with PAM off, including Alpine Linux’s default sshd setup, a side effect of a 2016 fix for CVE-2016-6210; Previdian saw the first attempt less than nine hours after disclosure and confirmed root on an Alpine honeypot, and attempts reached 20 from 13 IPs in nine countries by November 8. Debian and Ubuntu also patched CVE-2026-109518 (CVSS 7.7), an APT bug found by Max Justicz that lets an on-path attacker on HTTP package sources swap a verified package for one whose maintainer scripts run as root whenever download retries are on, the default since APT 2.3.2 (fixed in APT 3.0.4 for Debian 13 and 2.6.2 for Debian 12, with Ubuntu 22.04 through 26.10 updated November 4), and no exploitation of it was reported in the articles read.

2Exploited edge gear: SonicWall MFA bypass, a Zyxel zero-day behind an old fix, and Plex2Shell on QNAP

CISA added SonicOS CVE-2026-19868 to its exploited-vulnerabilities list with a November 20 federal deadline; the SSL-VPN flaw lets an attacker who already holds valid credentials for another login method skip SAML-enforced MFA on Gen7 and Gen8 firewalls running 7.3.3-7015 or 8.2.2-8015 and older (fixed in 7.3.4-7013 and 8.2.3-8015 along with six other bugs not flagged as exploited), though SonicWall has shared no attack details or indicators. Zyxel disclosed on November 3 that its June patch for CVE-2026-7273 missed the bug actually behind attacks since about August 17, CVE-2026-107441 (CVSS 8.8, unauthenticated command execution through the web interface of ten GS1900 switch models, fixed in firmware ending .3C0), with 996 switches in 48 countries compromised and their configs and root hashes taken in activity The Hacker News ties to Kapibala and its JITTERLY backdoor, while QNAP warned that the four-flaw Plex2Shell chain (CVE-2026-96652, -96654, -96655 and -96656) is compromising Plex Media Server on its NAS devices without credentials, fixed in Plex 1.43.3 or later.

3Trusted providers as the way in: cPGuard, Qubole, Ethersphere and Crédit Mutuel Arkéa

Attackers who signed up for a free cPGuard trial under a false identity got command execution on OpsShield’s billing portal, stole publishing keys and pushed a fake root-level update on November 1–2 that about 2,950 customer Linux servers ran during a roughly 70-minute window, exposing root SSH keys, password hashes and MySQL root passwords, and Wiz said an attacker holding credentials for the AWS account Qubole uses assumed dormant but still-trusted IAM roles at multiple customers from October 14 to 26 to read Lambda variables, Secrets Manager and SSM values, Okta and Entra secrets, and whole S3 buckets. On November 9, stolen CI credentials let attackers ship a wallet-keystore stealer and backdoor in Ethersphere’s @ethersphere/bee-js 13.3.0 on npm (live about 75 minutes, with valid provenance) and in a signed, notarized Swarm Desktop 0.56.2, and French bank Crédit Mutuel Arkéa said attackers reached it through an unnamed service provider’s platform to make fraudulent transfers that ZachXBT puts at about $133 million, part of which has been blocked.

4Breach disclosures pile up and Japan funds an emergency response

Japan announced a ¥10 billion (about $63 million) emergency cybersecurity package on November 6, expanding its new active cyber defense powers and adding joint Defense Ministry, police and National Cybersecurity Office teams, an AI-driven METI expert team by the end of November and cyber rescue teams, after a run of incidents the AP links to KDDI (12 million affected), So-net (2.6 million records) and Nikkei (1.8 million), and on November 9 Iris Ohyama said a hacked external cloud service may have exposed data on up to 7.7 million Iris Plaza customers and took the store offline. U.S. filings reported November 9 put Amplifon Americas’ January breach at 1,332,826 people (health and insurance data, plus Social Security numbers for some) and Frontline Education’s August breach of school staff and student records at 3,085,266, and DigiCert disclosed on November 6 that a business system left reachable from the internet by a February 2025 migration let an attacker with a self-created account read customer contact data and ID documents from June 27 to July 2, 2026, without any ability to issue certificates.

5Follow-up: refrigeration attacks draw a U.S. advisory and a French attribution to Iran

Since the November 3 report, CISA, the FBI, HHS, USDA and DOT warned in a joint November 6 advisory that attackers are forcing grocery, cold-storage and industrial refrigeration controllers into heating and defrost cycles, with fire and food-and-medicine safety risks, and told operators to take controllers off the internet, segment them and change default passwords, while three French Blood Establishment sites had cold rooms reportedly pushed toward 110°C overnight November 1–2 through Coolworld-maintained equipment, with no blood products lost. On November 9 France’s C4 attributed the October attacks on more than 700 grocery stores, which started at least 15 fires, to the IRGC’s Shahid Kaveh unit (CyberAv3ngers) and proposed new EU sanctions, and SecurityScorecard separately found Copeland XWEB PRO controllers, likely entered through the CVE-2026-25085 login bypass, sending spam to 1,041 targets and relaying proxy traffic in a botnet it has not linked to the European attacks.
