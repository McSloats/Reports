Top 5 cyber trends of the past week

1PHP2Shell and a week of parser and runtime RCE flaws

PHP shipped emergency fixes on October 21 (8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 and 8.2.35) for two unauthenticated remote-code-execution flaws called PHP2Shell (CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619), after Amazon saw targeting on October 17, Cloudflare logged attempts rising to millions by October 22, and a Perplexity researcher recorded working exploitation in honeypots on October 24; a further unpatched PHP bug (CVE-2026-115560) has an interim mitigation of setting session.upload_progress.enabled=0. Other widely used components were hit the same week: Node.js fixed the Brotli decoder flaw behind “Node2Shell” (CVE-2026-111497, October 21; 24.22.1 confirmed to reject the test data), Ruby patched three regex-engine bugs (4.0.9, 3.4.12, 3.3.13) behind four critical GitLab RCE CVEs, and Pillow 13.0.0 fixed several image-parsing flaws, none of which were reported as exploited in the articles read.

2Follow-up: agentic AI intrusions are now being measured in the wild

Since the October 12 report on attackers using AI agents, CrowdStrike published research on October 21 covering four actors whose agent frameworks were left exposed, including one that breached at least 15 Argentine healthcare organizations with a modified PentestGPT, an Indonesian actor running 819 mostly public skills with no custom orchestration, and a Russian-speaking actor driving Claude Code through a seven-step workflow, and Cybersecurity Dive reports CrowdStrike saw agentic intrusions triple in the second half of 2026 versus the first. Separately, NorForge found exposed control panels for the open-source Cairn tool being used against more than 1,200 shop sites at over 100 companies from about October 2, with roughly a 10% success rate, about $5 per task and about $5,000 in total token spend.

3Broken trust plumbing: eMudhra CA keys and hijacked Tor exit relays

Eight eMudhra intermediate CA keys (six for TLS, two for code signing) from an April 30, 2025 key ceremony were weak enough to be recovered from the public certificates in under a second, so Chrome and Firefox blocked them within hours of the October 17 report, Apple and Microsoft followed on October 19 and 20, about 30,000 customer certificates need replacing, and eMudhra said on October 22 it will exit the global TLS business; whether anyone abused the keys is unknown. Separately, attackers controlled more than 300 Tor exit relays between October 1 and 5, redirecting SSH traffic to 12 interception servers to capture passwords (7.46% of exit capacity at the October 4 peak), with one relay operator tracing the intrusion to a reused password and the Ebury backdoor.

4Network and security management platforms exploited: Arista VeloCloud and FortiSIEM

CISA added Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator’s SQL injection flaw (CVE-2026-114625, CVSS 9.9) to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on October 23, with Arista confirming active exploitation, fixes in 5.2.3.18, 6.1.3.10 and 6.4.2.12, and The Hacker News counting it as the third VeloCloud Orchestrator flaw exploited since July. Fortinet’s FortiSIEM 7.5.0 has a critical unauthenticated path-traversal-to-RCE bug (CVE-2026-106173, CVSS 9.4, gRPC port 27918, fixed in 7.5.1) with a public Horizon3.ai exploit, and the Canadian Centre for Cyber Security says it is being exploited, citing open-source reporting.

5SafePay ransomware advisory and fresh data-exposure fallout

The FBI and CISA, with German and UK agencies, issued an advisory on October 20 saying SafePay has hit at least 600 organizations since August 2024, gaining access through stolen credentials, brute force, VPNs and edge-device flaws (including CVE-2020-3259 and CVE-2023-20269), while Conduent agreed on October 16 to an $80 million settlement over an attack linked to the group. Meanwhile the State Bank of India confirmed on October 21 that 3,897 customer records appeared in a dark-web set a seller claims holds 375,000 rows, and OpenCode said a legacy-system breach on October 16 may have exposed provider API keys for 1.4% of its Console users, with no confirmed misuse in either case.
