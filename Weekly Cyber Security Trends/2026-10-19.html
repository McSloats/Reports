Top 5 cyber trends of the past week

1Edge and remote-access products are exploited faster than they can be patched

Ivanti Connect Secure zero-day CVE-2026-104935 (CVSS 8.6, fixed October 13, part of a three-bug chain whose privilege-escalation stage is still unpatched), PAN-OS CVE-2026-0322 (CVSS 8.6, root code execution, added to CISA's KEV on October 14 with an October 17 deadline) and Cisco Secure Email Gateway CVE-2026-76519 (CVSS 9.8, fixed in September but now exploited) all came under attack. Mandiant tied BeyondTrust Remote Support CVE-2026-102764 (CVSS 9.3) to the China-nexus group Armory Castle, active since August 29 ahead of an early-October patch, while SonicWall SMA1000 CVE-2026-102255 (CVSS 10.0) drew exploitation attempts within two days of disclosure.

2Microsoft sets another Patch Tuesday record, with AI cited as the cause

Microsoft's October 13 release fixed a record 1,413 CVEs (BleepingComputer counted 1,411), including more than 70 rated Critical and one exploited zero-day, the Windows Shell Mark-of-the-Web bypass CVE-2026-96951 (CVSS 8.8, CISA deadline October 16). Krebs reports that Microsoft credits large language models for the surge in bug discovery, and Tenable noted that this year's CVE total has already passed 4,000 with two months left.

3AI raises the speed of attacks on infrastructure and the theft of AI credentials

Booz Allen's lab found frontier AI models met all eight attack objectives against an industrial-control testbed, going from perimeter breach to action in the control zone in just over 16 minutes and moving a robotic arm (human sign-off was required for physical actions). On the criminal side, Bitsight saw forums selling autonomous hacking tools that run on stolen OpenAI, Anthropic and Google API keys, and OpenCode said an October 16 intrusion into a legacy system may have exposed provider keys for about 1.4% of Console users, with some users reporting unauthorized charges.

4Retail and food supply chains disrupted across Europe

Attackers hijacked refrigeration controllers at hundreds of French supermarkets on October 11–12, reportedly pushing temperatures up to 50°C and spoiling stock, with the manufacturer blaming outdated firmware. Ahold Delhaize took systems offline across the U.S., Netherlands and Belgium from October 11 (disrupting Albert Heijn distribution and online orders, cause undisclosed), and UK fashion group TFG London (Phase Eight, Hobbs, Whistles) detected an incident the same day with data exposure still unknown.

5Software supply chain: CI/CD secrets and package registries

The latest GhostAction wave pushed credential-stealing GitHub Actions workflows at more than 30,000 repositories via about 1,900 compromised accounts, spiking October 8–9 and attempting to extract 3,174 secrets before GitHub began blocking the workflow on October 9. Separately, Bloom Security found npm's Trusted Publishing matched repositories by name rather than numeric ID, leaving over 1,500 packages (more than 1 million monthly downloads) open to takeover until a fix announced October 2.

TSA watch: cybersecurity news mentioning TSA

DHS inspector general finds weak access controls at TSA

SC Media reported on October 16 that a DHS inspector general audit found weaknesses in TSA's IT access management, including improper handling of privileged accounts, service accounts created without proper approval, and former-employee accounts not consistently disabled. The brief says these gaps could let attackers compromise TSA's network and sensitive data, and cites a separate report that a contractor could extract passenger driver's license and passport images from biometric screening equipment without TSA's knowledge.

This item was found only on SC Media; no other reviewed outlet surfaced a TSA cyber story dated October 12–19. The brief credits Biometric Update as its original source, which was not read for this report.
